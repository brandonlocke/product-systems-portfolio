# Portfolio design brief

The portfolio helps a hiring manager understand what Brandon can own, how he makes product decisions, and what the evidence establishes.

## Content and hierarchy

- Bring selected work directly after the introduction. The homepage presents three primary cases; supplementary work remains discoverable through a clearly labeled disclosure.
- Open each case with contribution, collaborators, delivery status, and observed results. Keep the problem, constraints, decisions, and evidence connected.
- Distinguish program activity, launch evidence, delivery evidence, and business impact. State the measurement period and relevant limits alongside claims.
- Preserve existing case URLs. Give missing pages a clear route back to the work.
- Use plain language and concrete decisions. Remove repeated positioning statements and artifacts that add no explanatory value.

## Presentation and interaction

- Use whitespace, alignment, and proximity to establish relationships. Keep line lengths comfortable and actions easy to recognize.
- Retain the Newsreader and Instrument Sans typography. Use a restrained paper, ink, and green palette, with readable contrast and visible focus states.
- Reveal supporting models on request using native, keyboard-accessible disclosure controls. Keep essential role and result information visible.
- Use explicit status labels rather than implied certainty. Do not equate a pilot send with conversion or a cohort association with causal lift.
- Preserve responsive navigation and reading order. Respect reduced-motion preferences; avoid attention-seeking animation.

## Verification and publication

Run the production build, app type checks, and rendered-route smoke tests. Review desktop and narrow-screen layouts, navigation, disclosure, and fallback behavior. Publish through the existing GitHub-to-Cloudflare deployment path for brandonlocke.co.
