# Portfolio design brief

The portfolio helps a hiring manager understand what Brandon can own, how he makes product decisions, and what the evidence establishes.

## Content and hierarchy

- Bring selected work directly after the introduction. The homepage presents three primary cases; supplementary work remains discoverable through a clearly labeled disclosure.
- Open each case with contribution, collaborators, delivery status, and observed results. Keep the problem, constraints, decisions, and evidence connected.
- Distinguish program activity, launch evidence, delivery evidence, and business impact. State the measurement period and relevant limits alongside claims.
- Preserve existing case URLs. Give missing pages a clear route back to the work.
- Use plain language and concrete decisions. Remove repeated positioning statements and artifacts that add no explanatory value.

## Presentation and interaction

- Use whitespace, alignment, and proximity to establish relationships. Keep line lengths comfortable and actions easy to recognize.
- Use Manrope as one variable font family for headings and body text. Create hierarchy through scale, weight, spacing, and color. Use a restrained paper, ink, and green palette, with readable contrast and visible focus states.
- Use 18px equivalent body text, 15–16px supporting copy, and at least 13px portfolio metadata at the default browser size. Demo content uses 14–16px text with 12px metadata. Keep font sizes relative to the browser default, and reflow narrow layouts instead of shrinking type. Demo panels grow with content; controls remain readable and usable at normal zoom.
- Show one interactive product reconstruction near the start of each case. Use illustrated interface states and working local controls to explain the behavior. Reveal deeper supporting evidence on request using native, keyboard-accessible disclosure controls. Keep role, result, and delivery status visible.
- Use explicit status labels rather than implied certainty. Do not equate a pilot send with conversion or a cohort association with causal lift.
- Preserve responsive navigation and reading order. Motion starts only when requested, with pause, replay, restart, and step controls. Pause timed playback when a demo leaves view, the tab is hidden, or another demo is activated. Reduced-motion preferences replace timed playback with manual stepping and disable decorative transitions.
- Label every demonstration as a reconstruction with illustrative data. Interactions change only local demo state; they do not create real orders, bookings, surveys, or tickets.

## Approved references

- [Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/): editorial reading rhythm and visual explanations beside the ideas they clarify.
- [Paul Stamatiou's Stocketa case](https://paulstamatiou.com/stocketa): product evidence connected to decisions, with explicit delivery status.
- [Brian Lovin](https://brianlovin.com/): a direct, personal introduction and recognizable groupings of information.
- [Seán Halpin](https://www.seanhalpin.xyz/): a recognizable visual identity for each project within one coherent system.
- [Dan Mall on portfolio pieces](https://danmall.com/learn/process-craft/creating-portfolio-pieces/): make the individual's contribution and judgment explicit.

Adapt these patterns to a product management portfolio. Interactive reconstructions explain the rules and behavior Brandon helped define; they do not imply sole ownership of interface design. Homepage covers use restrained project-specific colors and carry a reconstruction label. SVG illustrations of vehicles, service parts, and shops add concrete visual context without requiring heavy image or video assets.
